# Who makes it from the academy to the Premier League?

*A plain-English summary. The full method is in `youth-to-senior.md`.*

## The big picture

We followed **1,092 academy players** from the U18 Premier League and Premier League 2 (the under-21 league) between 2022 and 2025, then checked who went on to play in the Premier League by October 2026.

- **120 of them (about 1 in 9)** have played at least one Premier League minute.
- **Only 19 (about 1 in 57)** have played 1,000 minutes or more, roughly 11 full games.

Among those 19: Kobbie Mainoo, Lewis Hall, Myles Lewis-Skelly, Nico O'Reilly, Lewis Miley, Tyler Dibling, Ethan Nwaneri and Jack Hinshelwood.

## Finding 1: being young for your level matters more than any stat

The clearest sign that a player would make it wasn't how many goals he scored or passes he made. It was **how young he was compared with the players around him.**

The players who made it were usually **about eight months younger** than their teammates and opponents. Their clubs had moved them up early, for example a 16-year-old playing in the under-21 league. Two-thirds of their youth minutes came in the under-21 league, compared with under half for everyone else.

Think of it like a school pupil skipping a year. A player who already holds his own against older boys is a better bet than one who dominates kids his own age.

## Finding 2: the players who made it did more with the ball going forward

At youth level, the future Premier League players:

- **shot more**, and hit the target more,
- **dribbled past opponents more**,
- **got into the penalty box more**,
- **created more chances** and scored more.

Defensive numbers (tackles, interceptions, clearances) **made no difference.** Players who made a lot of tackles at youth level were no more likely to make it than anyone else.

For defenders who made it, what stood out was **winning their duels and headers** and **passing the ball well**, not how many tackles they made.

## Finding 3: some skills carry over to the Premier League, others don't

We looked at the 21 graduates who have played enough Premier League football to measure.

**Carries over: dribbling.** Players who were good at beating a man in youth football are still better at it than most Premier League players in their position. Winning free kicks, scoring and tackling also tended to rank players in a similar order at both levels.

**Doesn't carry over: being on the ball a lot.** As teenagers these players took more shots, made more passes and had more touches than their peers. In the Premier League they're about average on all three. At youth level you can be the main man; in the Premier League everyone is good.

*With only 21 players, treat this part as a strong hint, not proof.*

## Finding 4: the "nearly men"

We also looked at **31 players who were among the very best at youth level**, aged 21 or older now, who **haven't played a Premier League minute.** Most were defenders and central midfielders.

**What they had in common with the players who made it:**
- they played regularly in the under-21 league,
- they passed the ball accurately,
- the defenders won a lot of duels and headers.

**What they were missing:**
- **They were older.** They were about 18 and a half when the study began, compared with about 17 for the players who made it, and usually older than the players around them. Part of why they looked so good is that they were up against younger players.
- **Their numbers came from defending and safe passing**: clearances, recoveries, long balls. The players who made it stood out for **carrying the ball forward**: dribbling, passing into the final third, getting into the box.

Some of these players are probably doing well in the Championship or abroad. This study only counts the Premier League.

## What to keep in mind

- **The numbers are small.** Only 19 players became Premier League regulars in this period, so these are patterns, not rules.
- **Making it isn't only about talent.** Which club you're at (breaking into Manchester City's team is harder than breaking into Southampton's), injuries, loans and the manager's choices all matter, and this study doesn't measure them.
- **Being moved up young is partly a sign that the club already rates the player**, so it isn't a magic ingredient on its own.
- **Not much time has passed.** Players from the 2024-25 youth season have had at most two seasons to break through. Some will make it later.
- **No youth xG.** The data shows how often a player shot, but not how good his chances were.

**Data:** the Premier League's own match statistics, free on its website, covering every U18 Premier League, Premier League 2 and Premier League match from 2022-23 to 19 October 2026.
